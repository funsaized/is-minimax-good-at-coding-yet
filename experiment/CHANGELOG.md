# Iteration 42

A reader's "annotavi" mark in the bottom-left margin and an ink-drop at the foot of the chapter spine.

## Added

- **Lectoris nota** — a quiet reader's gloss in the bottom-left margin
  (`"annotavi · I have marked it"`, vermilion italic Latin with an
  ink-soft English gloss) that types itself in once the reader has
  understood the chapter (`intellexi` done). A small caret beneath
  points toward the colophon, and a soft warm halo behind the mark
  suggests where the reader's quill rested on the parchment.
  Completes the folio's four-corner marginal scheme (top-left,
  top-right, bottom-right were already inhabited; bottom-left was
  empty) and the chapter's Latin dialogue chain
  `quaeritur → respondetur → relege → intellexi → annotavi → legi → explicit`.

- **Reader-mark pip on the chapter spine** — once `annotavi` has
  finished writing itself, a single small vermilion ink-drop falls
  to the bottom of the chapter spine, like the reader's mark has
  descended the chapter's axis and landed at the colophon. A subtle
  vermilion-tinged glow joins the existing gold spine glow, so the
  spine's sealed state now carries both the chapter's warmth and
  the reader's mark. Additive: it never replaces the existing
  pinprick column, sealed hairline, or travelling pulse.

## Choreography

The full chapter cadence now reads:

1. The page asks — the question mark draws itself on load.
2. The reader engages — the chapter spine pulses.
3. The answer writes itself in vermilion dropcap + italic.
4. The reply writes itself, ornament between.
5. The footnote writes itself (relege · without a reader, silence).
6. Intellexi settles — the reader has understood.
7. **Annotavi lands in the bottom-left margin** — the reader has marked it.
8. **A single ink-drop falls to the foot of the spine** — the mark
   reaches the colophon.
